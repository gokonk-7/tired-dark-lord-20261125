You are the forty-seventh. I keep count in the prophecy's margin — forty-seven chosen ones, forty-seven swords, forty-seven speeches about dawn. The first ten I fought properly. The next twenty I fought politely. These days I offer tea. The kettle is older than your kingdom, the cups chipped like all I own. My scheme, since you ask: the eclipse engine beneath the floor, three centuries' stolen starlight, one lever. I have not pulled it. Pulling it means war, and war means waking before noon. So drink. Go home, tell them I am terrible, and return when the kettle boils again.
